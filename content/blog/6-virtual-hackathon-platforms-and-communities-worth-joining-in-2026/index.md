A virtual hackathon is a time-boxed building event where teams form, write code and submit a working project entirely online. Judging usually happens through a recorded demo and a project page. The format lets a developer in Philadelphia build with teammates in Lagos or Seoul over a single weekend or a 30-day window.

<!--more-->

We put this list together for a mixed group of readers. Some of you want a free virtual hackathon to try this month. Some of you are early-career developers or bootcamp graduates looking for an online hackathon for beginners. Others are CSR program managers and donors who want to know where builder time and sponsor money actually go. The same handful of platforms and communities serves all of you, so we describe each one by what it does and who it fits.

Our selection criteria were plain. Each resource had to list or run online events you can join in 2026. It also had to be open to people outside a single company, and it had to have a track record we could check. We left out tools that only help organizers host an event, because this list is about finding and joining one. The table below names every resource in the order it appears.

> If you only open one resource, start with Devpost. It is the platform where most large hackathons are hosted, and [Devpost reports more than 4 million registered builders](https://litslink.com/blog/virtual-hackathons). Its filters for online events, themes and prize types make it the fastest way to find a 2026 event that matches your skills.

## Devpost: the main directory for online hackathons

[Devpost](https://devpost.com) is a hackathon platform where organizations post challenges and developers register, form teams and submit projects. Each project gets a public page with a description, a demo video and a code link. That page doubles as a portfolio entry long after judging ends. Companies use Devpost to run sponsored challenges, and many of those challenges are fully online.

The scale of these online events can be large. [When Bolt ran its 2025 online hackathon, more than 130,000 people registered](https://litslink.com/blog/virtual-hackathons). For a first-timer, that size has two sides. You get exposure to big prize pools, and you also compete against many teams. Prize structure appears to affect follow-through: one research summary found that [events with $10,000+ prize pools see 75–80% submission rates](https://wal.sh/research/online-hackathons/), while [events with non-monetary prizes see 50–60% submission rates](https://wal.sh/research/online-hackathons/).

- Filters for online, in-person, theme and open-or-invite-only events
- Public project pages that work as a portfolio
- Team-finding tools for solo registrants
- Many free virtual hackathons with sponsor-funded prizes

**Best for** Developers who want a wide choice of online events, and sponsors who want to see how corporate challenges are typically structured and judged. Beginners should filter for events that explicitly welcome first-time participants and pick a smaller one first.

## Major League Hacking: student leagues and global hack weeks

Major League Hacking is the official student hackathon league. It partners with member events, publishes a season calendar and runs its own online programs, including global hack weeks built around a theme such as AI or open source. MLH also sets a code of conduct that member events follow, which matters to parents, schools and sponsors checking whether an event is safe and well run.

The reach is wide. [Major League Hacking reported 250,000 participants globally in 2024](https://wal.sh/research/online-hackathons/). By 2026 the calendar still mixes in-person and online events, so builders can practice online before traveling to a campus event.

- Season calendar of member hackathons, with online events marked
- Global hack weeks you can join from anywhere
- Shared code of conduct across member events
- Workshops and mini-challenges aimed at newer developers

**Best for** Students, bootcamp graduates and early-career developers who want an online hackathon for beginners with clear rules and structured support. Sponsors who want to reach student talent also start here.

## Cerebral Valley: AI builder events

[Cerebral Valley](https://cerebralvalley.ai) is a community and events hub focused on artificial intelligence builders. It lists and co-hosts [hackathons](https://codephilly.com/blog/8-ways-to-get-real-value-from-online-hackathons-as-a-builder-or-sponsor), demo nights and meetups centered on AI models, agents and developer tools. Many events are tied to a specific model provider or tool, so participants often get credits or direct access to the sponsoring team.

AI hackathons in 2026 frequently run longer than a weekend. One example from another organizer shows the shape: the [IBM Agentic AI Hackathon 2025 drew more than 450 participants in 225 teams from 25 countries over 30 days](https://www.spektrasystems.com/blog/best-hackathon-platforms/). Builders tracking AI events can use a hub like Cerebral Valley to find formats similar to that one.

**Best for** Developers already comfortable with APIs who want to build agent or model-driven projects, and AI companies looking to put their tools in front of active builders.

## Techadelphia: Philadelphia tech community

Techadelphia is a Philadelphia tech community that connects local developers, founders and companies through events and shared resources. It is useful for virtual hackathon builders in a specific way: online events are easier when you already know people to team up with. Local communities give you those people.

If your interest leans toward a theme, local groups also surface specialist events. Our [6 Ways](https://codephilly.com/blog/6-ways-to-get-more-from-cybersecurity-hackathons-in-philadelphia) piece on security events and our [9 Healthcare Hackathon Tips](https://codephilly.com/blog/9-healthcare-hackathon-tips-for-builders-mentors-and-sponsors) list both draw on this kind of local network, and [What Is Hack](https://codephilly.com/blog/what-is-hack-for-humanity-and-how-do-social-good-hackathons-work) for Humanity covers social good formats that often run online.

**Best for** Philadelphia developers who want local contacts before joining a global online event, and regional sponsors who want visibility in the city's tech scene.

## NASA International Space Apps Challenge: a global open-data event

The [NASA International Space Apps Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_International_Space_Apps_Challenge) is an annual hackathon where teams build solutions to challenges using NASA open data. It runs at local sites around the world and also accepts virtual participation. During the pandemic it held a fully virtual special edition. The 2025 event had [114,094 participants across 551 locations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_International_Space_Apps_Challenge).

- Free to join
- Published challenge statements with linked datasets
- Virtual and local site options
- Global and local judging rounds

**Best for** Builders of any level who want a free virtual hackathon with real public data, and educators or donors who want students to work on science problems.

## C.O.D.E Philadelphia: weekly sessions and quarterly hackathons

C.O.D.E Philadelphia publishes this article. C.O.D.E (Community of Developers & Engineers) is a volunteer-run nonprofit founded in 2024. According to the organization's own figures, it has 5,700+ members on Meetup, 2,200+ followers on LinkedIn and 490+ active builders on Discord. Its core programs are in-person: weekly coding sessions and quarterly hackathons at venues like Pennovation Works, including the AI Agent Hackathon.

For virtual hackathon participants, the Discord community is the online piece. Members use it to find teammates, share progress and ask questions between events. Practicing weekly in person can also make a later online event easier, because you arrive with a team and a working setup.

- Weekly coding sessions where members code side by side
- Quarterly themed hackathons open to all skill levels
- Discord server with 490+ builders
- Sponsorship, donation and volunteer opportunities

**Best for** Philadelphia-area developers, designers and early-career builders who want steady practice and teammates, plus sponsors and donors who want to fund hands-on tech programming in the region.

> Want more builders in Philadelphia to arrive at their next hackathon ready to ship? Join a weekly session, volunteer at a quarterly hackathon, or sponsor and donate to C.O.D.E Philadelphia so more developers of every skill level get the practice and teammates that turn a weekend project into real experience.
