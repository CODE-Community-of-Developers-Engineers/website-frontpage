A hackathon coming up is a dated, time-boxed building event on your calendar: a few weeks out, a registration link open, and a theme announced or about to be. The weeks between seeing that date and walking into the venue decide most of what you get out of the weekend. This round-up covers that window and nothing else.

<!--more-->

I run C.O.D.E Philadelphia, a volunteer-run nonprofit with 5,700+ members on Meetup and 490+ active builders on Discord (per our own community counts). We hold quarterly [hackathons](https://codephilly.com/blog/8-ways-to-get-real-value-from-online-hackathons-as-a-builder-or-sponsor) at venues like Pennovation Works, plus themed events such as our AI Agent Hackathon. We see the same pattern every quarter. The people who prepare in small, specific ways ship more, find better teammates, and come back. The sponsors who show up early in the planning get far more from their support than the ones who send a logo the week before.

The tips below are written for several readers at once: Philadelphia-area developers and engineers, side-project builders, bootcamp graduates heading into a first or second event, and the corporate sponsors, CSR managers and donors who make these weekends possible. Each tip says who it is for.

> Act on this first: show up to one weekly coding session before the hackathon. You meet likely teammates, test your laptop setup in a real room, and learn the event theme from organizers directly. It costs one evening and removes the biggest weekend risk, which is arriving alone with nothing installed.

## 1. Meet your team at a weekly session before kickoff

**Form your team before the clock starts.** Team formation at kickoff eats the first hour or two of a hackathon. Strangers trade names, guess at each other's skills, and often split up by Saturday afternoon. If you already coded beside someone at a weekly session, you know how they work, what they are good at, and whether you enjoy the same kind of problem.

Our weekly coding sessions are in-person meetups where members code side by side in a low-pressure setting. They are the closest thing to a rehearsal. You can float an idea, see who leans in, and agree to team up without the pressure of a countdown. Builders who skip this step can still form teams at the event, and plenty do well. The ones who meet first tend to start writing code sooner.

- Attend at least one weekly session in the two to four weeks before the hackathon.
- Say out loud that you are going and ask who else is.
- Post your skills and the kind of project you want in the Discord channel for the event.
- Aim for a team that mixes skills: someone on the front end, someone on data or back end, someone who likes presenting.
- Swap contact details and agree on a chat channel before kickoff.

## 2. Prepare your environment, not your project

**Install everything now. Build nothing yet.** Most hackathons ask teams to start the project at kickoff, so pre-written feature code breaks the spirit of the event and often the rules. Setup is different. Time spent fighting a package manager, an expired API key or a broken local database on Saturday morning is time stolen from the build.

Participation is common across the field. The [2024 HackerRank Developer Skills Report showed 70% of developers had participated in hackathons](https://wal.sh/research/online-hackathons/), which means your teammates will likely have opinions about tools. Settle those choices early. A short checklist works better than good intentions.

- Update your operating system, editor and language runtimes a week ahead, not the night before.
- Create accounts and API keys for any cloud, model or mapping service you expect to use, and confirm the free tier limits.
- Make an empty repository with your team, add everyone, and test that each person can push.
- Pack a charger, a power strip if you have one, and any adapters your laptop needs.
- Save offline copies of documentation you rely on, in case venue Wi-Fi is slow.

If the theme is AI agents, as it was for our AI Agent Hackathon, a basic framework install and one working test call to a model is the right level of preparation. Agent logic, prompts and tools belong to the weekend.

## 3. Read the theme and rules like a brief

**The theme tells you what judges will reward.** Every themed hackathon has a brief, even when it is a single sentence. Read it the way a contractor reads a client request: what problem is named, who the user is, and what a finished demo should show. Teams that ignore the theme can build impressive work and still miss the prizes.

Finishing matters as much as ambition. [Across MLH events in 2024, 60–70% of registered participants submitted a project](https://wal.sh/research/online-hackathons/), so a meaningful share of people who register never reach the submission step. A scoped idea that matches the theme is what gets a team across that line. If you are drawn to a specialty track, field-specific advice helps: our [9 Healthcare Hackathon Tips](https://codephilly.com/blog/9-healthcare-hackathon-tips-for-builders-mentors-and-sponsors) cover clinical problems and privacy, the [6 Ways](https://codephilly.com/blog/6-ways-to-get-more-from-cybersecurity-hackathons-in-philadelphia) piece on cybersecurity covers scope rules for security builds, and [What Is Hack](https://codephilly.com/blog/what-is-hack-for-humanity-and-how-do-social-good-hackathons-work) for Humanity explains how social good judging differs.

- Write the theme on one line and agree as a team on what it means.
- Find the judging criteria and note which ones count most.
- Check rules on prior code, open-source libraries, AI coding tools and team size.
- Pick an idea you can demo end to end in the time given, then cut half of it.
- Plan the last two hours for the demo, not for new features.

## 4. Early-career builders: pick one skill to prove

**Go in with one thing you want to show you can do.** Bootcamp graduates and early-career developers often treat a hackathon as a test of everything they know. That spreads you thin. A better goal is one concrete skill: shipping a deployed API, wiring an interface to real data, or presenting a demo to judges without notes.

A single skill turns into a clear line on a resume and a specific story in an interview. It also tells your team where you fit, which makes you easier to recruit. Our quarterly hackathons are open to builders of all skill levels, and the weekly sessions give you a place to practice that skill before the event and keep going after it.

- Choose your one skill and tell your team at kickoff.
- Ask a more experienced teammate to review your part on Saturday, not Sunday.
- Keep the repository public after the event if the rules allow it.
- Write a short project summary the week after, while details are fresh.

## 5. Sponsors: commit while the event is still being planned

**Early sponsors shape the event. Late sponsors decorate it.** For corporate sponsors and CSR program managers, the useful window is weeks before the hackathon, when the theme, judging, mentors and challenge tracks are still being set. Support committed then can fund a challenge built around a real problem your engineers care about, or mentor time that changes how teams work.

C.O.D.E Philadelphia describes itself as a working community, not a networking happy hour. For a sponsor, that means your support goes to people building things in a room, and your engineers can meet them as mentors and judges, beside the code.

## 6. Donors and volunteers: back the weekend you want to see

**Small support before an event has a visible result.** Recurring donors and nonprofit supporters can see exactly what a hackathon produces: teams, demos and repositories built over one weekend. Giving before the event funds the practical pieces that let a first-time builder attend, like food and a working venue, at no cost to them.

Volunteering is the other route. Hackathons need people for check-in, mentoring, timekeeping and judging. Experienced developers who volunteer often say the mentoring hours are the part they remember. Sign up early so organizers can plan shifts around you.

### What hackathons are coming up?

In Philadelphia, C.O.D.E Philadelphia runs quarterly hackathons at venues like Pennovation Works, plus themed events such as the AI Agent Hackathon. Dates and registration go out on our Meetup group and Discord first. Weekly coding sessions run between hackathons, so there is always an event within a few days.

### What hackathons are upcoming in 2026?

C.O.D.E Philadelphia holds a hackathon every quarter in 2026, with themes announced ahead of each one. The quarterly rhythm continues into 2027. Following our Meetup group is the most reliable way to see each date as it is confirmed.

### Is joining a hackathon worth it?

For most builders, yes, when they prepare. A hackathon gives you a deadline, teammates and a finished demo in one weekend, which is hard to get from solo side projects. It is most worth it when you arrive with a team or a skill goal and keep building afterward at weekly sessions.
